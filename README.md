**He was born with the sky inside him,**
with wind for blood
and a wild, unmeasured horizon
calling his name.

**No chains were placed upon his wrists**
when he first opened his eyes.
Yet slowly, softly,
the world taught him the weight of iron.

**First came a name,**
then a number,
then a house with walls,
a clock with teeth,
a calendar crowded with red marks.

**You must.**
*You should.*
*You have to.*

**And he learned to carry**
what was handed to him—
a family, a future, a mortgage of dreams,
promises made before he understood
how expensive a promise could be.

**He ran from one duty to another,**
sometimes willingly,
sometimes with his heart dragging behind him
like a tired dog on a rope.

**He called it life.**

**But somewhere beneath the noise,**
a small, green longing remained.

**He did not want a throne.**
He did not want a mansion
or applause rising like smoke.

**He wanted to be a tangerine.**

Just a tangerine—

**hanging quietly from a slender twig**
deep in a green and nameless jungle,
where no clock knew his name
and no hand reached out to claim him.

**He wanted to wake**
when the first rain arrived,
each drop striking his skin
like a blessing that asked nothing in return.

**He wanted sunlight**
to pour over his shoulders,
golden and warm,
without turning itself into a deadline.

**He wanted the wind**
to move him without commanding him,
to make his leaves tremble
without telling him where to go.

**No résumé.**
No rank.
No expectation.

**No unfinished email**
waiting inside the morning.

**No question—**

*What have you achieved?*

**Only the patient language of trees,**
the deep breathing of wet earth,
the distant cry of a bird
disappearing into emerald silence.

**He wanted roots**
without imprisonment.

**A branch**
without a cage.

**To belong somewhere**
without belonging to anyone.

**And when the monsoon came,**
he wanted to surrender completely—
to let the rain wash away
every borrowed ambition,
every *should*,
every *must*,
every invisible hand
that had shaped him into a man
he never quite wished to become.

**Perhaps, he thought,**
freedom was never the absence of roots.

**Perhaps freedom was this:**

to be held by the earth
without being owned by it;

to drink the rain
without owing the clouds;

to ripen slowly
beneath the sun,

**until one day**
he could fall—

not because someone demanded it,
not because the world had decided
his time was over,

**but simply because**
he was ripe,

**and the earth below**
was ready to receive him.
